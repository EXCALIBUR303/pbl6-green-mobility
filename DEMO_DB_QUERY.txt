STEP 1 — Session vs. Cookie (20 sec)
------------------------------------------------------------------
1. On the login page, tick "Remember my email", log in.
2. Click your name (top right) -> Logout.
3. Point out: your email is still pre-filled on the login page
   ("Filled in from your last visit") even though you were just
   logged out. Say: "The cookie survived logout; the session didn't
   — that's the difference between the two."

------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 2 — Browse (10 sec)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Log back in. On "Browse Slots", point at any capacity bar and say
this is a live query against MySQL, not hardcoded.

------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 3 — Overbooking prevention (the main event — 60 sec)
------------------------------------------------------------------
The "Academic Block -> Food Court" slot is pre-set to exactly
1 SEAT LEFT (2 of 3 booked). Do NOT book it before the actual demo.

1. Click "Book" on that slot -> Confirm booking.
   -> Succeeds. Slot now shows "Full" on the browse page.
2. Try to book it again:
     Easiest: open a second browser tab, go to Browse Slots,
     click "Book" on the same (now full) slot.
   -> It redirects back with "Sorry, that slot just filled up."
     (The Book button is also disabled once full, so mention you're
     hitting the confirm page directly to prove the SERVER blocks it,
     not just a disabled button in the UI.)
3. Switch to VS Code, open SlotDAO.java, show tryReserveSeat():
     UPDATE vehicle_slots SET booked_count = booked_count + 1
     WHERE slot_id = ? AND booked_count < capacity
   Say: "This check and the write happen as ONE atomic database
   operation, so two people booking the same last seat at the same
   instant can't both succeed — only one UPDATE actually changes a
   row, the other affects zero rows and we treat that as full."

OPTIONAL — prove it at the database level (if using TablePlus):
  Run this query before step 1 and again after step 2:
     SELECT slot_id, route, booked_count, capacity
     FROM vehicle_slots WHERE slot_id = 3;
  Shows booked_count going 2 -> 3, and staying at 3 after the
  rejected attempt (not incrementing past capacity).
